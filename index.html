About

Understanding People. Shaping Safer Technology.

I am a behavioural scientist, psychologist, researcher and international speaker with more than two decades of academic and professional experience studying how people think, communicate, make decisions and change behaviour.

My career has spanned academia, psychological practice, research and entrepreneurship, including roles as an assistant professor, researcher and practicing psychologist. Across more than two decades of psychological practice, research and work in education, I have supported thousands of young people and families, giving me a deep understanding of human behaviour in real-world environments.

Today, I apply behavioural science to some of the most important challenges emerging at the intersection of people and technology — responsible AI, AI validation, online safety, cybersecurity and defence, gaming, digital communities and wellbeing. My work focuses particularly on how AI interprets human behaviour, how people respond to intelligent systems, and how behavioural evidence can help make technology safer and more trustworthy.

I founded Felixa Health to translate this work into practical approaches for understanding behaviour and creating safer online environments. Alongside my research, I advise organizations, lead workshops and speak internationally on the human side of AI, digital safety and behaviour change.

Speaking

Big Questions About AI. Grounded in Human Behaviour.

Dr. Ewa Antczak brings more than two decades of behavioural science, psychology and research to some of today's most urgent questions about AI, trust and digital safety. Her talks make complex ideas understandable, provocative and practical — helping audiences think differently about how technology affects people and how we can make it safer.

Signature Keynotes

01

Can We Trust AI to Understand Human Behaviour?

AI increasingly interprets emotions, intentions and behaviour. But how do we know when its judgments are right? A thought-provoking exploration of what AI understands about people — and what it still gets wrong.

02

Beyond Accuracy: Why AI Needs a Behavioural Reality Check

AI can perform well on technical benchmarks and still fail in the complexity of real human situations. This keynote explores why human-grounded validation may be one of the missing layers in responsible AI.

03

From Moderation to Prevention: Creating Safer Digital Spaces

What if we could identify behavioural escalation before an online interaction becomes harmful? Dr. Ewa explores how behavioural science and AI can help move digital safety from reacting to harm toward preventing it.

04

The Human Factor in Cybersecurity & Defence

Technology may detect threats, but people still interpret information, communicate, make decisions and act under pressure. This talk examines behavioural risk, resilience, human–AI interaction and decision-making in high-stakes environments.

05

The Psychology of Trust in the Age of AI

Why do people trust some technologies and reject others? Drawing on behavioural science, this keynote explores trust, uncertainty, human oversight and what organizations need to understand as AI becomes part of everyday decisions.

The Psychology and Technology Behind Safer Gaming

Seminar
Workshop
15/30/60 min Talk
Panel

We are living in a transformative era where emerging technologies and online platforms have redefined how people connect, collaborate, and play. While these spaces offer valuable opportunities for creativity and community, they also expose users to harmful behaviors including aggression, hate speech, exclusion, and harassment. Ensuring psychological safety within digital environments has become a pressing societal concern.

Traditional reactive approaches—bans, reports, moderation—are often too late, too punitive, and lack consistent health metrics.

Our team of behavioral scientists and AI experts developed an AI-powered behavioral analytics system that integrates machine learning with principles from behavioral psychology. Validation research shows this AI-powered tool aligns with human judgment across multiple languages, scales to all 24 official EU languages, and costs 50% less than traditional moderation tools.

Reducing Toxicity in Online Gaming Spaces

Seminar
Workshop
15/30/60 min Talk
Panel

Did you know that 87% of gamers are bullied online? Toxicity is now the leading reason players quit gaming. What was once designed as a space for creativity, collaboration, and play has, for many, become a source of stress, exclusion, and harm. The need to ensure psychological safety in gaming environments has never been more urgent—for developers, studios, and communities alike.

Who Should Attend
Game Developers Esports Organizers Community Managers Trust & Safety Professionals Educators Policymakers

This session is designed for game developers, esports organizers, community managers, trust-and-safety professionals, educators, and policymakers who shape the future of digital play. Together, we will explore how behavioral science and AI-driven technologies can reduce toxicity, enhance player well-being, and strengthen trust across gaming ecosystems.

Protecting emotional safety online doesn't just promote mental health—it preserves the freedom to play, compete, and create without fear of abuse or silencing.

Key Topics Covered
Behavioral Science AI-Driven Technologies Player Well-being Empathy Gaps Emotional Regulation Community Culture Transformation

What You'll Discover

Drawing on recent validation studies and large-scale player data, I will use live gameplay examples and AI demos to show how psychological factors (empathy gaps, emotional regulation, dark triad traits) and environmental drivers (anonymity, toxic reward loops, competitive modeling) contribute to harmful behaviors.

We'll also examine how real-time behavioral analytics and AI-guided interventions can transform community culture from reactive moderation to proactive prevention.

Expected Outcomes
Safer Gaming Environments Enhanced Player Retention Reduced Toxicity Proactive Prevention Strategies Healthier Communities Improved Mental Health

Attendees will leave with practical strategies to create gaming spaces where players feel safe, respected, and empowered to engage authentically without fear of harassment.
